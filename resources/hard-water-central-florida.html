Why Central Florida Water Is So Hard

The Floridan Aquifer runs through limestone. Here's what that costs you.

Nearly all of Central Florida's drinking water comes from the Floridan Aquifer, one of the most productive aquifers in the country. It also sits in limestone and dolomite, rock made largely of calcium and magnesium carbonate. Water moving through it dissolves those minerals and carries them to your tap.

That's the whole story. Hard water here isn't a treatment failure or a sign your utility is cutting corners. It's geology. Utilities test for health-based contaminants, and hardness isn't one. The EPA calls it a secondary standard, meaning aesthetic. Nobody is required to remove it before it reaches you.

Hardness gets measured in grains per gallon. Above 7 gpg is hard. Above 10 is very hard. Most of our service area falls between 10 and 18, and parts of Lake County test higher.

You notice the cosmetic damage first: spots on glassware, film on the shower door, soap that won't lather, laundry that comes out stiff. The expensive damage is the part you can't see. Calcium drops out of solution when water gets heated, so your water heater is where scale builds fastest. That layer of scale insulates the heating element from the water, so the unit runs longer to hit the same temperature and dies years early.

The fix is a softener. Ion exchange swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium or potassium. The technology is old, well understood and reliable. The decisions that matter are matching the unit to your household's hardness and water use, and deciding whether you also want carbon filtration for the chloramine taste.
