The Rotten-Egg Smell in Well Water

Hydrogen sulfide is why your well smells like sulfur. Here's how it gets fixed.

If your well water smells like rotten eggs, it's hydrogen sulfide. Bacteria living in the low-oxygen environment of the aquifer feed on naturally occurring sulfate minerals and release the gas as a byproduct.

Your nose is remarkably good at finding it. People detect hydrogen sulfide at roughly half a part per billion, far below any level that poses a health concern. That's why the smell can be overpowering when the lab report calls the concentration low.

Here's a useful test. If you only smell it on the hot side, the culprit is probably the magnesium anode rod in your water heater reacting with sulfate. Swap it for an aluminum-zinc rod and the smell usually goes. If you smell it on both hot and cold, it's coming from the well itself and needs treatment where the water enters the house.

That treatment is oxidation followed by filtration. Convert the dissolved gas into elemental sulfur, capture the solid in a media bed, then backwash the bed on a schedule. The same process handles dissolved iron, which is why sulfur and iron problems get solved together.

One warning. Sulfur odor sometimes travels with bacterial contamination, so test for both at once. If coliform comes back positive, add a UV sterilizer to the system.
